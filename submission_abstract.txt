Platforms detect coordinated inauthentic behaviour (CIB) with signals designed for human troll farms: copy-pasted text, synchronised posting and repeated co-activity. Which of these survive when the coordinating accounts are LLM agents? We inject 30-account LLM agent swarms of six sophistication levels, from template bots to orchestrated, detector-aware and self-seeding swarms, into 33.7k real Taiwanese Threads/Facebook/PTT messages from 25k accounts. Agents match real accounts in platform and activity and promote a fictional tea brand. Once agents are persona-conditioned, lexical, temporal and embedding detectors rank swarm accounts at or below chance (AUROC 0.14–0.48); inverting the best recovers ranking (0.83–0.86) but not precision (average precision, AP ≤0.04). A per-account LLM monitor degrades from 0.86 to 0.74. We propose PayloadConv, a population-level detector that extracts who is endorsed from every message and scores targets that many accounts endorse uniformly, unprompted and across unrelated threads. Against non-adaptive swarms promoting this new brand, PayloadConv reaches AUROC 0.92–1.00 and AP 0.69–0.99 at a 1% base rate; this replicates on a frozen held-out window (AP 0.88–1.00) and with swarms from another model family (Gemini; AP 0.71–0.98). Promoting an established brand lowers AP to 0.49–0.88, far above non-payload detectors, and an account-level score on the same extractions stays at AP ≤0.07 on S4. It is not robust: a self-seeding swarm drives it to chance (0.44). A provenance-aware variant restores ranking (AUROC 0.91–0.95) but not precision (AP 0.12–0.44). For swarm oversight, the shared goal is the invariant to monitor and the population is the unit.
